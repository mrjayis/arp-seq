# arp-seq

A syncable **arpeggiator** feeding a **2–6 step sequencer** that changes the pitch and
volume of each arpeggiated note. It runs in the browser (Web Audio + Web MIDI) and has
no dependencies.

```sh
npm start        # serves http://localhost:8080 (ES modules and Web MIDI need http, not file://)
npm test         # unit tests for the arp, sequencer and clock logic (Node 18+)
npm run bump     # bump the ?v= stamp on asset URLs; do this before each release
```

## How it works

```
held notes ──► ARPEGGIATOR ──► note ──► SEQUENCER step n ──► pitch + offset, velocity × volume ──► synth / MIDI out
                  ▲                             (n advances by one for every arp note, wrapping at length 2–6)
             clock (internal or MIDI clock in)
```

### Scenes
- **Save scene** stores everything as it is now under a name: all the sounds, the arp sequence, mono sequence, progression, drum and break patterns in every slot, the song chain, mixer levels and effects. **Recall** brings it all back. **Update** overwrites a scene with the current state, and **Rename** and **Delete** do what they say. The scene you last saved or recalled is outlined.
- **Recall is live:** while playing, the scene comes in on the next bar's downbeat without stopping (its row blinks while it waits). When stopped it applies at once. **Undo recall** switches back the same way.
- Not part of a scene: the look (theme), which windows are folded, MIDI ports and clock source, master volume, the built-in synth switch and the keyboard octave and chord root, since those belong to the device or the moment. Your drum samples and loaded break are stored once in the browser rather than in each scene, so a scene uses whichever ones are loaded.
- Scenes live in this browser. **Export…** downloads them all as a file, as a backup or to move them to another device. **Import…** adds the scenes from such a file to the ones you have.

### Recorder
Record samples (the **Rec** chip in the top bar):
- **Source:** **This app** (resampling: the app's own sound, after the mix, or every pair when Outputs are separate), **Microphone / input** (any input, chosen in **Input**; the browser's echo cancelling, noise suppression and automatic gain are switched off so it's recorded as it is, or as near as the browser allows; the browser asks for permission the first time. On an iPhone the app switches Safari's audio session to play-and-record while it records and back to playback after, since a playback session isn't allowed the microphone. If it's refused, the message says why: not allowed (with where to allow it: on an iPhone, aA in the address bar, Website Settings, Microphone), busy in another app, or not found), or **Another tab or the system** (through the browser's screen sharing, which asks what to share: tick its "Share audio" / "Share system audio"; Chrome on a Mac can share a tab's audio, Windows the whole system's; the video is dropped at once).
- **Length:** **Free** records from the press to **Stop** (up to 2 minutes); with **Trim silence** the quiet before and after is taken off, keeping the attack. **1, 2, 4 or 8 bars** starts on the next bar line (starting playback if it's stopped) and stops exactly that long later, so a resampled loop is exactly its bars long, ready to slice. Takes from an input are shifted by its latency so they line up with the bars.
- A meter shows the level while recording. Then the take is drawn, and you can **Play** it, **Use as break** (it becomes the Breakbeat's break, saved with it; a bar-synced take sets Break is to its length unless you've trimmed it), **Put on row** of the **Your samples** drum kit (switching to that kit), **Put on track** (a slot S1–S8 of the Breakbeat's Samples, for Tracks 2–4 of its tracker), **Download WAV** (16-bit, stereo), or **Discard** it.
- **Trimming a take:** drag near either edge of its waveform to move that edge; the cut-off parts are drawn faint, and the start, end and length are shown. **Trim to sound** takes the quiet off both ends (keeping the attack); **Whole take** undoes the trim. Play, Use as break, Put on row, Put on track and Download all use the trimmed part, with tiny fades at the cuts so they don't click.

### Clear all
**Clear all** (top bar) puts everything back to how the app starts: every sound, sequence, pattern, progression, drum kit, effect, mixer setting and solo setting. It's kept: your scenes, loaded drum samples and breaks, the iReal Pro list, and what belongs to the device (theme, folded windows, MIDI, audio output, Volume). It asks "Sure?" first; while playing it comes in on the next bar, like a scene; and **Undo recall** in the Scenes window brings back everything as it was.

### Sections A–D
Every module with a pattern has four sections, **A**, **B**, **C** and **D** (the **Section** buttons in its window): the **Arpeggiator** (On/Off, Mode, including Coltrane solo and Bach invention, Rate, Octaves, Gate, Swing, the solo's Space, Bebop and Build, and the invention's Subject; Level and Latch stay as they are; Swing also sets the groove of the chord rhythm, straight drums and bass line), the **Sequencer** (its steps and length), the two **arp voices** (Oscillator 1's sound: wave, cutoff, shape, timbre, gate, resonance, attack, release and level; Oscillator 2's: on/off, its Sequence mode (Same, Inverted or Bass line), sound, wave, transpose, detune, level, shape, timbre, gate, cutoff and sync, resonance, Mod → Osc 1, attack, release, the upright bass's tone and decay, and the bass line's style and rate), the **Progression** (its chords, length, and the loaded song's title and style), the **Chord rhythm** (rate, steps, hits, rotate, Rhythm/Pad, Syncopate and Tie over), the **Mono Synth** (its sequence), and **Percussion** and **Breakbeat** with their pattern slots. A section you haven't used starts as a copy of the one you're on, ready to change; used ones are underlined. **Section** in the top bar switches every module at once, for a verse, chorus or bridge. While playing, a switch comes in on the next bar line (the button blinks until then); stopped, at once. Sections are saved with your settings and in scenes, and Clear all empties them.

### Song mode
String sections together into a song (the **Song** window).
- **Blocks:** the song is a row of blocks, each a section (A–D) for a number of bars: e.g. **A × 8, B × 8, A × 8, C × 4**. **+A … +D** adds a block of the length in **for** (1–16 bars). Tap a block's letter to change its section, **−**/**+** to make it a bar shorter or longer, **×** to remove it. Two blocks of the same section next to each other are one block. **Clear** empties the song.
- **Song On** starts playback if it's stopped and plays the blocks in order, looping round; the block playing is highlighted, with the bar it's on.
- **Whole sections** (on by default): each block switches every module to its section on its first bar line (the Arpeggiator, Sequencer, both arp voices, Progression, Chord rhythm, Mono, Percussion and Breakbeat): set up a verse in A, a chorus in B and so on with the Section buttons, then lay them out here. Off, only the Percussion and Breakbeat follow the song, playing their pattern slot each bar, as before.
- **Pattern slots A–D** in the Percussion and Breakbeat windows are their sections: you edit the selected one; **Copy to…** duplicates it. Each slot keeps its own variation too: the Percussion's **Evolve** and **Fill every**, the Breakbeat's **Evolve**, **Rolls** and **Reverse**. So a verse can play the break straight and the chorus evolve it with 8th-note stutters. Song mode uses each slot's own, whether or not it switches the windows to it.

### Arpeggiator
- **Level:** the overall level of the arpeggiator, both voices together. Each voice also has its own **Level** (in the Oscillator 1 and Oscillator 2 windows) for balancing them. None of these change the MIDI it sends.
- **On / Off:** switches the arp's sound (both voices, and its MIDI out). While off, the arp and sequencer keep stepping silently in time, so the sequencer display moves and chords or a pad set to Follow sequencer still follow it. The readout shows "(arp off)".
- **Modes:** Up, Down, Up/Down, Down/Up, Random (never the same note twice in a row), As Played
- **Bach invention** (Mode): a two-part invention in the manner of J. S. Bach's, over the chords. The arp plays the upper voice; set the Mono Synth's **Play** to Bach invention too and it plays the lower voice against it (either alone plays a single line). Each time you press Play there's a new **subject**: a bar with a turn of 16ths and then leaping 8ths, like the opening of the C major Invention, and a **countersubject** in 8ths moving against it. The form goes a bar at a time:
  - **Exposition:** the subject alone in the upper voice, then imitated an octave lower in the lower voice (on whatever chord comes next, so it can be a real answer at the dominant) while the upper voice plays the countersubject.
  - **Development:** the subject or its **inversion** in either voice against the countersubject, and **episodes** where the subject's first four notes are sequenced down a step at a time over a bass walking down in quarter notes.
  - **Cadence:** each section ends with the upper voice running down and the bass on root and fifth, then long notes on the downbeat after it, and the next section opens with the subject alone again. A section is one pass through the progression, or as many passes as make at least 8 bars.

  **Your own subject:** type it in the **Subject** box as note names, e.g. `C5 D E F D E C G5- C6- B5- C6-` (the C major Invention's opening). Each note is a 16th; each `-` after a note holds it a 16th longer (`G-` is an 8th, `G---` a quarter); `r` is a 16th's rest (`r-` an 8th's). Octave numbers are optional: without one a note goes to the octave nearest the note before (the first to octave 5), so write it where a leap goes the far way (`G5` above C5, where plain `G` would be the 4th below). Or press **Record** and play it on the keyboard (on screen, computer keys or MIDI): each note is typed in with its octave, then add `-`s for the longer notes and press Record again (or edit the box) to use it. A subject of half a bar or less is answered a 5th higher to fill the bar, as in the C major Invention; a longer one holds its last note to the bar line; past a bar, it's cut at the bar line. It's kept as its steps along the scale (its letter names), so it fits every chord it meets, and the first statement starts on your first note when that's in the chord; the countersubject is written against it, and everything else (imitation, inversion, episodes from its first four notes, cadences, the counterpoint check) works from it. The box remembers it; **Random** goes back to a new subject each time you press Play. A typo shows in red and the last good subject carries on.

  **Try a Bach progression** (in the Invention row) sets it all up in one tap: the Baroque circle of fifths in C (C | F | Bm7♭5 | Em | Am | Dm | G | G7), the Mono Synth on the lower voice, 84 BPM, playing from the top. The Progression's **Preset** menu has it too, with **Bach: tonic and dominant (C)** (C | G7 | C | F | Dm | G7 | C | G7) and **Bach: minor circle (Am)** (Am | Dm | G | C | F | Bm7♭5 | E | E7♭9). Each ends on the dominant, so the cadence falls there and resolves as the progression comes round.

  **Counterpoint:** every note is checked against the rules of two-voice writing (as taught from Fux's species counterpoint on, and as Bach keeps them), and if the planned note breaks one, the generator plays the nearby alternative (a scale step either side, a chord tone close by, or at the start of a statement the octave) that breaks the fewest, the least badly; a clean planned note always stands, so the lines keep their shape. The rules, most serious first:
  - no crossing of the voices; no parallel 5ths or octaves, nor 5ths or octaves by contrary motion
  - only consonances (unison/octave, 3rds, 5th, 6ths: the 4th counts as a dissonance in two voices) on beats 1 and 3
  - elsewhere, a dissonance only as a passing or neighbour note, reached by step
  - no hidden 5ths or octaves (similar motion into one with the upper voice leaping)
  - no tritone leaps, 7ths or leaps over an octave; a leap of a 4th or more is followed by a step back the other way
  - no more than three parallel 3rds or 6ths in a row; the voices within two octaves; unisons only at the start and end; no repeated notes where the line should move

  Over 200 inventions on the Bach circle of fifths, this took parallel 5ths and octaves from about 800 to under 10, strong-beat dissonances from 1,700 to about 60 and unprepared dissonances from 5,300 to under 100, and made contrary motion more common. The subject itself follows the leap rule too: its leaping 8ths step back after a leap of a 4th or more, as in the C major Invention.

  In a key (the Key setting, or the progression's on Auto), the lines keep to the key's scale as Bach does, not each chord's mode: in C, B natural over F and F natural over G. A chord tone from outside the key replaces the note it alters, so V in a minor key brings its leading note (harmonic minor). The material is kept as moves along the scale, so it fits each chord it meets: beats 1 and 3 are always chord tones (reached the way the line is going, so sequences keep falling), beats 2 and 4 can be passing notes, and the lower voice always stays under the upper. It plays straight 16ths whatever the Rate (the tempo sets the speed: about 70–100 BPM suits it), slightly detached as on a harpsichord, with the subject's entries a little louder. Oscillator 2 follows the upper voice as usual (or plays its bass line), and Transpose on the Mono Synth moves the lower voice.
- **Coltrane solo** (the last Mode): instead of arpeggiating, the arp plays a solo line over the chords in John Coltrane's pentatonic manner. It builds four-note cells, mostly **1-2-3-5** (the "Giant Steps" figure: B C♯ D♯ F♯ over B major), played up, down or turned around (5-3-2-1, 3-2-1-5, 1-5-3-2). For each chord it picks a major pentatonic that colours it, as Coltrane did and as teachers such as Jerry Bergonzi later set out: over a major 7th, the pentatonic on its root, 5th or 2nd (the 2nd's gives the Lydian ♯11); over a minor 7th, on the ♭3, 4 or ♭7; over a dominant 7th, on the root, 4 or ♭7; over an altered or ♯9 dominant, on the ♭5; over a half-diminished chord, on the ♭6 or ♭2. Sometimes it plays the chord's own 1-2-3-5 instead (1-2-♭3-5 on minor chords), and diminished chords get their arpeggio. Every chord change starts a new cell, so with two chords a bar at Rate 1/8 you get one cell per chord. Each cell starts a small step on from the last note in the direction the line is going, and turns round at the edges of its range (G3–A5), so it reads as one long line. **Space** sets how much the line breathes between phrases; at 0 it never stops (sheets of sound, especially at 1/16). Around the pentatonics, the bebop language he came out of, set by **Bebop** (0 is pure pentatonic):
  - **Enclosures:** the last notes before a chord change surround a tone of the new chord (its 3rd or 7th most often), a scale step above and a half step below, so it lands right on the change.
  - **Bebop-scale runs:** the chord's scale plus one chromatic passing note (the major 7th on a dominant, ♯5 on a major chord, major 3rd on a minor), so the chord tones fall on the beats; mostly falling.
  - **Chromatic runs:** half steps into a chord tone, only within a chord (never a chromatic note on the downbeat of a change).
  - **Motifs:** a phrase's opening figure comes back later, moved to fit the chords it lands on (sequenced through the changes) and sometimes upside down, and a new phrase sometimes opens with it again.

  - **Phrase endings:** a phrase usually comes down on a downbeat: when it's done and the next step is a chord change or beat 1 or 3, it plays one last note there, a chord tone of the new chord close to the line, held for about a beat, and then breathes. An enclosure often leads into that last note. Now and then a phrase still stops just before the beat instead.

  **Build** shapes the solo over 1, 2 or 4 choruses (a chorus is once through the progression, or 8 bars without one), then starts again like the next soloist. Early on it plays lower, softer, with more breath and mostly pentatonics; as it builds it climbs about two-thirds of an octave, plays harder and in longer phrases, uses more runs, and near the top side-slips: a cell a half step outside the chord that resolves straight back in. **Steady** keeps it in the middle. These are his methods, not his recorded licks; every line is generated fresh. The chord comes from the progression, or from the keys you hold. Rate, Gate, Swing, Level and Oscillator 2 all still apply; sequencer steps switched off still rest, but their pitch offsets are ignored.
- **Rate:** 1/4D (dotted quarter), 1/4, 1/8D (dotted 8th), 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16D (dotted 16th), 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32, placed on a 24 PPQN grid so rate changes stay in time
- **Octaves:** 1–4 · **Gate:** 5–100% · **Swing:** 0–75% (delays off-beat steps)
- **Latch:** keeps notes after you let go; a new chord replaces them. The sustain pedal (CC64) also toggles latch.
- **Tempo:** 20–300 BPM, with tap tempo

### Mono synth (101-style)
- **Little keyboard:** two octaves under the steps for writing the sequence directly. Tap a key and it goes in at the cursor, with no need to arm Write. Legato, Rest, Tie and Back work with it as usual. **−** and **+** move it by an octave (C0 to C8). While the sequence plays, the key of the note now sounding is marked.
A monophonic bass/lead voice with its own step sequencer, playing in time with everything else.
- **Play: Coltrane solo:** instead of its sequence (which is kept), the Mono Synth plays a solo line over the chords: the same soloist as the Arpeggiator's **Coltrane solo** mode (pentatonic cells, bebop enclosures and runs, motifs, phrases ending on the downbeat, the build), at the Mono's **Rate**, with its sound and filter. **Legato** makes the line glide from note to note like a 101 lead (a phrase's last note still lets go into the breath); **Transpose** moves it by octaves; **Gate** sets how short the notes are. It has its own line, separate from the arp's, so the two can play at once. Space, Bebop and Build are shared: they're in the Arpeggiator's Solo row, which shows whenever either is soloing.
- **Voice:**
  - **Oscillator:** Saw, Pulse (Width, plus PWM from the LFO), Sub (−1 oct square, −2 oct square or −2 oct narrow pulse) and Noise, each with its own level.
  - **Filter:** a 24 dB resonant low-pass with Cutoff, Resonance, Env amount and Key follow.
  - **Envelope and VCA:** ADSR envelope; the VCA follows the envelope or acts as a simple gate.
  - **Modulation:** LFO for vibrato and filter wobble. **Portamento** Off / On / Auto (Auto glides only into legato steps).
- **Writing a sequence (up to 64 steps):** arm **Write** and play notes on the Keyboard window, computer keys or MIDI. Each note fills the next step, and the arpeggiator is left alone while Write is armed.
  - **Rest** adds silence, **Tie** holds the previous note, and with **Legato** on, notes slide in without retriggering.
  - **Back** deletes, and clicking a step writes from there.
- **Playing:** **Rate** (default 1/16), **Gate**, **Transpose**, and **Keys transpose**: the keys you play shift the sequence live, with C3 meaning no change.
- **Output:** **Level**, Bass sends in the Effects window, and an optional MIDI out channel.

### Arp voices (west-coast style)
The arp has two monophonic voices: **Oscillator 1**, and **Oscillator 2**, which plays the sequence as-is or inverted. Each voice is a complex oscillator feeding a low-pass gate:
- **Timbre** folds the sine core (a triangle wavefolder). At 0 it's a pure sine; turning it up adds bright, glassy harmonics.
- **Shape** crossfades between the folded sine and a second **Wave**: Saw, Square, Triangle, Spike (a narrow pulse) or Sine.
- **Gate**, with three modes:
  - **Combo** strikes a vactrol-style low-pass gate. **Cutoff** sets brightness, loudness *and* how long the note rings (from ~40 ms closed to ~2.5 s fully open), so there's no separate decay control. The arp's Gate time doesn't affect it.
  - **VCA** works the same way without the filter.
  - **LP** is filter only: the note holds for exactly the arp's Gate time.
- **Resonance** for the gate's filter.
- **Attack** and **Release** (each oscillator has its own: Oscillator 1's in its window, Oscillator 2's in the Envelope row of its window; a sound saved before they were separate gives Oscillator 2 the same values): Attack is how quickly each note fades in. Release adds tail: on top of the ring in Combo/VCA, or as the fade after the gate in LP. At their minimums the voices sound as before.
- **Bass line** (Oscillator 2's third Sequence choice): instead of following the arp's steps, oscillator 2 plays its own bass line over the chords, the way a play-along app such as iReal Pro has its bassist do. It plays on the beat, following the progression (or, with the progression off, the keys you hold), even with the arp switched off. **Style:**
  - **Walking:** a quarter note a beat. Each chord starts on its root; the last beat before the next chord leads into it by a half step from below or above, or from its fifth; the beats between move by step through the chord's scale (Dorian for minor 7ths, Mixolydian for 7ths, altered for 7alt, and so on), with chord tones on the stronger beats. The line carries on from its last note and keeps to the bass's range.
  - **Two-feel:** root and fifth in half notes.
  - **Bossa:** root on 1, the fifth on the "and" of 2, held over.
  - **Roots:** the root once a bar.
  - **Rate**, as the arpeggiator's: the bass line's rhythm, **1/1** to **1/16** with dotted and triplet values. Walking plays a note per value (1/8 gives an 8th-note walking line, 1/8T triplets), still starting each chord on its root and leading into the next; Roots repeats the root at it (a pulsing 8th-note rock or disco bass); Two-feel alternates root and fifth at it. A dotted value's leftover goes to the chord's last note. Off-beat 8ths and 16ths take the arpeggiator's **Swing**. **Auto** keeps each style's own rhythm (Walking in quarters, Two-feel in halves, Roots once a bar). Bossa keeps its own pattern, so the Rate is greyed out for it.
  - **Auto** picks from the style of the iReal Pro song you loaded: Walking for swing (and anything else), Bossa for bossa, samba and Latin styles, Two-feel for ballads, Roots for rock, pop, funk and even-8ths styles.
  Choose **Sound: Upright bass** for the full effect (Transpose −12 puts it in the bass's real register). The bass line is written from E2 up, so with Transpose 0 on the West-coast sound it plays an octave higher, like a synth bass. MIDI Out Ch sends it on oscillator 2's channel.
- **Mod → Osc 1** (in Oscillator 2's panel): oscillator 2 modulates oscillator 1 with **AM** or **FM**, using its own pitch for each step (Same/Inverted, Transpose, Detune) and its own Wave.
  - **Depth** is the AM amount, or the FM index (0–8).
  - Off-step tail slides move the modulator too.
  - It still works with oscillator 2 switched Off, which makes oscillator 2 a silent modulator.
- **Sync cutoff** (Oscillator 2, on by default): Oscillator 1's Cutoff drives both voices.
- **Monophonic:** each new note on a voice cuts off that voice's previous note, with a 3 ms fade so there's no click. Chords stay polyphonic.

### Arr (automatic arranger)
- **Arr** (next to Tap): on the next bar line it mutes a random selection of the sections that are switched on (Arpeggiator, Chord Rhythm, Mono Synth, Percussion, Breakbeat), then changes the selection every **1 / 2 / 4 / 8 bars** (the menu beside it). At least two sections always keep playing, and a section that was muted is likely to come back next time, so the track breathes. Muted windows and their jump-bar chips are crossed out, and the button shows how many are out (**Arr −2**). Press **Arr** again for a new arrangement on the next bar. It starts the transport if needed.
- **Cancel** brings everything back at once and stops the arranger. Arr mutes the same way Solo does, so effect tails ring on, and it works alongside Solo.

### Sync
- **Tuning** (Clock window): **Equal** is ordinary tuning. **Just (5-limit)** and **Just (7-limit)** are *adaptive* just intonation. Every pitched synth (both arp voices, the AM/FM modulator, chord rhythm, pad, sub, FM sounds and the mono synth) is retuned chord by chord to pure ratios. The notes are *sweetened* together: each one has a few pure-ratio choices (a whole step can be 9/8 or 10/9, a minor 7th 9/5 or 16/9, and so on), and the tuning picks the combination that beats least across the whole chord and anything else sounding with it. Arp, mono and held notes outside the chord are then fitted to what's already sounding: major thirds 5/4 (−13.7 cents), minor thirds 6/5 (+15.6), fifths 3/2 (+2), and so on. The chord is the progression's current chord, or with the progression off the root is worked out from the keys you hold, in any inversion. With the progression on, each chord's root is itself tuned to a pure ratio above the key's tonic (worked out from the whole progression), so neighbouring chords keep their shared notes still: in C → Am, the C and E don't move. The tonic stays on its equal-tempered pitch, so nothing drifts. Diminished chords are built from two pure minor thirds. Held pad notes, a held (tied) mono note and a ringing arp note or tail (both oscillators, and the AM/FM modulator) glide to their new tuning when the chord changes, without retriggering, and syncopated early chords are tuned to their own root. **7-limit** also uses the "barbershop" 7th (7/4, −31 cents) and a 7/5 tritone. MIDI out stays in equal temperament.
- **Pure** (with Just tuning, on by default): removes the deliberate detuning that makes even perfectly tuned notes beat: Detune 2, Osc 2's detune, the FM sounds' detuned layers and vibrato, the mono synth's vibrato, and the chord chorus. Your settings for those are kept and come back when Pure is off or the tuning is Equal.
- **Key** (with Just tuning): the home note that chord roots are tuned against. **Auto** works out the key from the whole progression: the major or minor scale that fits its chords best, preferring a key whose home chord is in it, so ii–V–I comes out in C. Set it yourself to override that. A set key also applies with the progression off, to chords you hold, and it moves along with Modulate.
- **Readout:** the display at the top shows the **Tuning**, the **Key** it's anchored to ("(auto)" when it comes from the progression), and the **Chord** it's following now, with how far that chord's root is tuned from equal temperament (e.g. **Am −15.6¢**).
- **Humanize** (Clock window): loosens the quantization of every section. Notes land a little early or late, within ±20 ms at 100% but never more than a fifth of a step, so fast notes keep their order. Levels vary by up to ±25%. Each section is moved on its own, and every drum hit and chord note gets its own variation, so the parts don't drift together. It applies to the arp, chord rhythm, mono synth, percussion (MIDI out included) and breakbeat. At 0 everything is exactly on the grid.
- **Internal:** a look-ahead scheduler on the audio clock, kept running in background tabs by a Worker timer. Pressing a key starts the transport.
- **MIDI Clock In:** follows 0xF8 ticks and Start/Continue/Stop, and shows the tempo it detects. *Play* starts it by hand for sources that never send Start.
- **Send MIDI clock:** sends clock plus Start/Stop to the selected MIDI output when running on the internal clock.
- **Steadier playback** (Sound & MIDI window): for screen recording, or a slow or busy device, where the sound would crackle and the timing stutter. It uses a bigger audio buffer (so the sound engine has slack when the device is busy; keys you play respond a little later), schedules notes further ahead (about a third of a second instead of an eighth, so a stall on the page doesn't make them late), and updates the display more lightly (playback highlights and meters about 15 times a second instead of 60). In a test with the page blocked 150 ms out of every 250, it took notes scheduled too late from about half to none. The buffer is set when the sound starts, so switching it on or off while playing asks for a reload; the rest applies straight away. It's remembered on this device.
- **Audio out** (Sound & MIDI window): where this app's sound goes, the **System default** or another output device, such as a virtual audio cable (BlackHole on a Mac, VB-CABLE on Windows) to record it in a DAW like Ableton Live while everything else on the computer stays on your speakers. It needs Chrome or Edge (other browsers play to the system default). The browser only shows a page its output devices once it has microphone permission: **List outputs** asks for it, only to read the devices' names, and nothing is recorded. The choice is remembered on this device (not in scenes); if that device is unplugged the app plays to the default and says so.

### Using it with Ableton Live (or another DAW)
It runs in the browser beside Live (Chrome or Edge, for Web MIDI) and talks to it over a virtual MIDI port: the **IAC Driver** on a Mac (Audio MIDI Setup → MIDI Studio → IAC Driver → Device is online) or **loopMIDI** on Windows.
- **Tempo:** in Live's Link, Tempo & MIDI settings turn on **Sync** for that port's output; here set **MIDI In** to the port and the Clock window's Sync to external. Live's Play then starts this in time. (Or the other way round: **Send MIDI clock** here and Sync on the port's input in Live.)
- **Notes into Live:** set **MIDI Out** to the port and give each part a **MIDI Out Ch**; in Live, MIDI tracks set to that port and channel, monitoring In, play and record them on Live's instruments.
- **Sound into Live:** set **Audio out** to a virtual cable and record an audio track whose input is that cable.
- **Each module on its own track:** set **Outputs** to **Separate per module** with a multichannel output chosen in Audio out (BlackHole 16ch on a Mac, or an audio interface or virtual cable with several outputs on Windows). Each module and effect then has its own stereo pair: **1–2 Arp** (both voices), **3–4 Chords**, **5–6 Mono**, **7–8 Percussion**, **9–10 Break**, **11–12 Delay**, **13–14 Reverb**, **15–16 Harmonizer**. In Live, give each audio track that device as input and pick its channel pair (Live's Audio input settings may need those channels switched on). With fewer than 16 channels the last pair takes the rest (the window shows the layout); on a stereo output it stays one mix. Everything is post-fader as in the mixer (Level, mute, solo and Arr still apply); the Volume sets every pair; the master compressor is left out so each track arrives clean. A web page can only play to one device, which is why it's done with channels of one device rather than several devices.

### Progression (meta sequencer)
- **Clear** (next to Preset): empties the progression, every chord a rest, 4 long, and forgets the loaded song's title and style and turns Modulate off. Keys you hold still play. It asks "Sure?" first.
- Program **1–8 chords** with the buttons, or up to **128** with the **Chords** box. Each slot has a root, a chord type and a length in beats (1–8, or longer for a chord held over several bars from a chart). The types are Maj, min, min7, maj7, 7, dim, sus4, and the jazz chords: sus2, aug, add9, 6, 6/9, maj9, maj7♯11, maj7♯5, min6, m(maj7), min9, min11, m7♭5, dim7, 7sus4, 9, 13, 7♭9, 7♯9, 7♯11, 7♭5, 7♯5 and 7alt, plus Rest. The progression loops, locked to the clock (internal or MIDI clock in).
- **iReal Pro…** loads a tune's changes from iReal Pro. In iReal Pro, share a song or playlist and export it as an iReal Pro file (a web page, .html), then **Open file…** it here (on an iPhone, from the Files app), or drop it on the window. You can also paste an `irealb://` link, such as one copied from the iReal Pro forums. A playlist shows as a list you can search; tap a song to load it. The chart is played once through its form: repeats with 1st, 2nd and 3rd endings, "3x" repeats, bar repeats, then D.C. or D.S. back, on to the coda or stopping at Fine. Each chord gets its share of the bar as iReal spaces it (in Giant Steps, two beats each, or a whole bar), a chord held across bars becomes one slot, and N.C. is a rest. Chord qualities iReal has that aren't in the list above become the nearest type (13♭9♯11 plays as 7♭9, for example). Slash chords whose bass is a chord tone become that inversion. Chords keep the chart's spelling (B♭7, not A♯7). The song's tempo is used when the chart has one. The progression turns on, and the window shows the song's title. **Giant Steps** is built in as an example. The last list you imported is remembered on this device; **Clear list** forgets it.
- **Inversions:** each slot also has an inversion: **Root**, **1st**, **2nd** or **3rd** (for four-note chords). An inverted chord shows its bass note as a slash chord, e.g. G/B.
- **Register:** each slot can also be moved **Oct −2** to **Oct +2** from the progression's Octave, for example a low C, then F an octave up. With Smooth or Nearest-tones voicing, the chord is voiced as usual and then moved, so it lands exactly that many octaves away.
- **Modulate:** changes key every **1–8 rounds** (a round is once through the progression), **by** a half step, whole step, minor or major 3rd, 4th or 5th, up or down. The steps add up, so +2 every round goes C → D → E → F♯…, but the total is folded back within a tritone of the written key (−5 to +6), so it never drifts out of range. That means some changes go down instead of up, for example the 5th key in that example drops to G♯ below. The header shows the current key. Everything that plays the progression follows: the arp, chord rhythm, pad, sub, and Syncopate's early chords (which use the key of the round they push into). With **All synths** on (the default), the other synths change key too: the Mono Synth sequence, and any keys you hold or latch for the arp and chords. Turn it off to keep those in the written key. Stop and Play start again from the written key.
- **Voicing:** **As set** plays each chord in the inversion you picked. **Smooth (inversions)** keeps the first chord as set, then gives every later chord the inversion (in its octave or the one below) that moves the voices least from the chord before. C–G–Am–F becomes C – G/B – Am/C – F/C. The inversion menus then show what was chosen, and the loop voices the same way every time.
  - **Nearest tones** goes further: each voice of the previous chord moves to the closest tone of the next one, in any octave, so voicings can open out instead of staying in close position. When a 7th chord adds a voice, the new tone goes wherever it moves least. Voices stay within about an octave and a half of the first chord, notes never double, and ties go to the narrower voicing. ii–V–I (Dm7–G7–Cmaj7) becomes D F A C → D F G B → C E G B. With plain triads it often lands on the same chords as Smooth, which is already the shortest move there.
- The current chord feeds the **arpeggiator** (and oscillator 2) and the **chord rhythm** layer. Keys or chord pads you hold are **added** to it.
- **On** starts the transport. Stop and Play restart the progression from slot 1.
- **Octave** sets the chord register. **Preset** loads I–V–vi–IV, ii–V–I, I–vi–ii–V, i–VI–III–VII or i–iv–v–i.
- Edits take effect on the next clock pulse, so you can change chords while it plays.

### Chord rhythm
- **Live filter:** **Cutoff**, **Resonance** and, for the FM sounds, **Brightness** change notes that are already sounding: held pad notes, chords played on the chord synth's MIDI channel, and rhythm hits still ringing (once their pluck has finished). So you can sweep the filter over a held chord. Aftertouch stays on top. The mono synth's filter already worked this way.
A separate layer that repeats the **held chord** in a Euclidean rhythm while the arpeggiator (and oscillator 2) keep playing on top.
- **Mode: Rhythm / Pad.** Rhythm plays the Euclidean hits below. **Pad** holds the chord, always on, for as long as there are notes (keys, latch or the progression). When the chord changes, only the notes that change fade out and swell in; shared notes carry on. **Attack** and **Release** set the swell and the tail. It uses the same voice, sub, filter and chorus.
- **Sound: Analog / FM E.Piano / FM Pad.** Analog is the two-wave voice with PWM and filter. The FM sounds are DX7-style: sine operators frequency-modulating each other, without the filter (its controls grey out). Sub, Level and Chorus still apply.
  - **FM E.Piano:** a 1:1 "body" pair whose brightness fades as the note rings, plus a 14:1 "tine" pair that gives the metallic strike. Notes decay on their own, low notes longer than high ones. **Brightness** sets the modulation depth (softer playing is darker too), **Tine** the strike, and **Decay** how long middle C rings. In Rhythm mode the end of the Gate damps the note, so a long Gate lets it ring.
  - **FM Pad:** three slightly detuned operator pairs (body, an octave up, and a 3:1 shimmer) whose brightness swells in with the note, with a gentle vibrato that eases in. It works best in Pad mode, where **Attack** and **Release** shape it and Follow sequencer glides it like the analog pad. **Brightness** sets how glassy it gets.
- **Steps** (2–16), **Hits** and **Rotate**: the hits are spread as evenly as possible over the steps. For example, 3 in 8 = `x..x..x.`. A dot display shows where the pattern is.
- **Rate:** the chord layer has its own rate, separate from the arp's (default 1/8). It runs on the same clock.
- **Syncopate:** with the progression on, a chord hit on or after the last 8th note before the next chord plays that next chord early, pushing it ahead of the beat the way players anticipate a change. With C–G and a hit on the "and" of 4, that hit is already G. It only applies to hits that land there, so a rhythm with nothing in the last 8th is unchanged. Inversions, voice leading and keys you hold all apply to the pushed chord. It's for Rhythm mode; a pad changes on the beat.
- **Tie over** (with Syncopate): the early chord holds over the bar line instead of being struck again. If the rhythm also hits the downbeat, the first early hit lasts until the end of that downbeat hit's gate, and neither the downbeat nor any other early hit in between is re-struck. If there's no downbeat hit, the early chord plays its normal gate.
- **Sound:** two oscillators per chord note, **Wave 1** and **Wave 2** (default Saw and Square), with an equal-power **Blend** and **Detune 2** (±50 cents) for thickness. Also octave (±2), gate and level, plus an optional MIDI out channel.
- **PWM:** a chord wave set to Square becomes a variable-width pulse. **Width** sets it (5–95%), and a shared triangle LFO sweeps it by **PWM depth** at **PWM rate**. DC offset is removed so off-center widths don't thump.
- **Sub** (Off / Square / Sine, with its own level): one octave below the chord's lowest note. It goes through the chord filter but skips the chorus, so the bass stays tight.
- **Gate arp:** the arp plays only while the chord pattern is on a hit. With the defaults (chord at 1/8, arp at 1/16), each hit lets two arp notes through. The arp and sequencer hold their place during rests. Works even with the chord layer turned off.
- **Filter:** the chords have their own low-pass **Cutoff** and **Resonance**, separate from the main filter.
- **Chorus:** a two-voice stereo chorus on the chords only, with **Mix**, **Rate** and **Depth** controls.
- **Follow sequencer:** off by default, so the chord repeats unchanged. When on, the chord takes the pitch offset and volume of the step the arp is on. It never moves the sequencer forward. In **Pad** mode the held chord slides to each step instead of re-striking: **Glide** (pad row) sets how long the slide takes, from a near-instant jump to a slow 1 s bend, and a step at 0% volume fades the pad out until the next step.

### Score player
- **Load MIDI…** (or drop .mid files on the window) adds Standard MIDI Files to the **Piece** menu. They're kept in this browser. **Remove** takes the chosen piece off the list and **Clear all** removes every one (each asks you to click twice). Public-domain MIDI of Bach's two-part Inventions, chorales and much more is at mutopiaproject.org.
- Each part (a track, or a channel in a single-track file) gets its own synth: **Arp voice** (the west-coast voice, one note at a time), **Mono synth**, **Chord synth** (the chord layer's analog or FM sound, which can play chords), or **Off**. By default the first part goes to the arp voice and the second to the mono synth, which suits a two-part invention. One-note-at-a-time voices play each note until the next begins.
- It plays in time with the clock, starting on the next bar, alongside everything else. **Speed** (½× to 2×) plays it slower or faster against the tempo, **Use its tempo** sets the tempo written in the file, **Transpose** moves it ±24 semitones, and **Play** sets what happens at the end: **This piece once**, **Loop this piece**, or a playlist of every piece in the menu, **all in order** (going round again after the last) or **shuffled**. Each piece starts on the bar line where the last one ended, and the status shows which one of how many is playing. Humanize and Solo apply as usual (each part is in the section of the synth it plays on).
- **Just tuning** sweetens the score too. With no progression or held keys, the notes sounding together in the score are the chord it tunes to, so the two hands of an invention are tuned to each other.

### Percussion
- **What's playing:** while it plays, the grid shows how the current bar differs from the pattern on screen. That covers **Evolve**'s changes and, in Song mode, a different slot. Added hits get a dot and outline, dropped hits are greyed out with a slash, and hits at a different level get a small dot. Stop clears the marks.
Three kits, all synthesized live with Web Audio (no samples). Each kit remembers its own pattern, preset and mutes.
- **Latin:** in the style of a 70s preset rhythm box (below).
- **808-style:** a long pitch-swept boom kick, a two-tone snare with noise, a multi-burst clap, rim, toms and cowbell, metallic hats and a cymbal from the 808's six square oscillators at their original pitches (the closed hat chokes the open one). The open hat and cymbal are tuned against reference 808 recordings: a ~0.7 s open hat centred around 8 kHz, and a bright splash over a darker 2–5 kHz ring that lasts about 3 s, plus its Latin section: low/mid/hi congas, claves and maracas. Presets: Electro, Boom Bap, House, Half-time, and all seven Latin rhythms. On the 808 the bongos move to the higher congas and the guiro to the maracas.
- **Linn-style:** a tight, clicky kick, a crisp snare, clap, hats, tambourine, cabasa, tom and cowbell. Presets: Synth Pop, Funk, Ballad, New Wave. It's a synthesized take on that early-80s character; the original machine used recorded samples.
- **Jazz:** a small-band kit played light: **Ride** (a stick ping over a long, dark wash), **Ride bell**, **Hi-hat** (played with the foot: a short "chick"), **Snare**, **X-stick** (cross-stick), **Kick** (a soft 18" bass drum, felt more than heard when "feathered"), **Tom** (floor tom), and brushes: **Sweep** (a swish across the head) and **Brush** (a tap). It plays on a **triplet feel**: in each beat's four cells, the first is the beat, the second the middle of the triplet, the third the swung "and" (two-thirds through the beat) and the fourth the last sixth, so the patterns swing like a drummer, not a shuffle. (The Swing slider doesn't apply to this kit; the feel already is the swing.) Presets:
  - **Swing:** the ride's "ding, ding-a ding, ding-a" with accents on 2 and 4, hi-hat on 2 and 4, the kick feathered on every beat, and the snare comping on the swung "ands" and triplets by chance (25–50% per hit), so every bar's comping is different.
  - **Swing (more comping):** busier comping, with the odd kick and floor-tom "bomb" on the "and" of 4.
  - **Swing (brushes):** a brush sweep on every beat, taps on 2 and 4 and the swung "ands".
  - **Two-feel:** the ride with the kick on 1 and 3 and a cross-stick on 4 now and then.
  - **Ballad:** brushes with a soft kick and a ride bell every fourth bar.
  - **Jazz waltz (3/4):** every row loops every three beats: the ride on 1, 2-and, 3, hi-hat on 2 and 3, the kick on 1, the snare comping.

  Raise **Evolve** for more variation, and the Mixer under the grid sets each sound's level and tuning. Use it with an iReal Pro tune, the walking bass line and the Coltrane solo for a whole quartet.
- **The jazz patterns on every kit:** Latin, 808-style, Linn-style and Your samples each have them too (**Jazz swing**, **Jazz swing (more comping)**, **Jazz swing (brushes)**, **Jazz two-feel**, **Jazz ballad**, **Jazz waltz (3/4)**), played on that kit's nearest sounds, with the same chance comping:
  - **808-style:** the ride on the cymbal, the foot hi-hat on the closed hat, the snare, rim, kick and low tom; maracas for brushes.
  - **Linn-style:** the ride on the closed hat (the classic drum-machine swing), cabasa on 2 and 4, snare and kick; cabasa and tambourine for brushes.
  - **Latin:** maracas as the ride, claves on 2 and 4, the high conga comping, the kick, low conga and the guiro scraping in place of a brush sweep.
  - **Your samples:** cymbal, closed hat, snare, rim, kick, low tom, cabasa and tambourine.

  (The 808 and Linn open hats are cut off by their closed hats, so the ride doesn't go there: the foot hi-hat on 2 and 4 would choke it.)
- **Feel** (every kit): **Straight** plays the grid as 16ths, with the Swing slider shuffling them; **Jazz swing** puts each beat's cells on the triplet, as above. Loading a jazz pattern sets Jazz swing and any other pattern sets Straight, but you can change it: Jazz swing on Synth Pop's hats, for example, swings them like a ride.

- **Your samples:** twelve rows (Kick, Snare, Rimshot, Clap, Closed HH, Open HH, Tom Hi, Tom Lo, Tamb, Cabasa, Cowbell, Cymbal) that you fill with your own recordings, such as your own drum machine.
  - Loading: **Load samples…** (or drop a folder's worth of files on the grid) matches each file to a row by the **first three letters** of its name: KIC/BAS → Kick, SNA → Snare, RIM/SID/SST → Rimshot, CLA → Clap, CHH/CLO/HHC/HIH → Closed HH, OHH/OPE/HHO → Open HH, TOM+H/L → toms, TAM, CAB, COW, CRA/RID/CYM. Short codes like BD, SD or CH1 work too, and a prefix shared by the whole batch (e.g. "LinnDrum " or "LD_") or a track number is skipped first. Unrecognised files fill empty rows; drop a single file on a row to replace it.
  - Hi-hats: names with the word open or closed after it ("Hat Open", "HH Closed") go by that word.
  - **Sample mixer** (under the buttons): shows which file is on each row and lets you put any loaded file on any row, swapping with what was there, or empty a row. Each row also has a **Level** (0–150%, double-click for 100%) and a **Tune** (±12 semitones in 0.1 steps, double-click for 0). Tune changes playback speed like the hardware's tuning knobs, so tuning down also lengthens the sound.
  - **Mixer** (Latin, 808 and Linn kits): the same **Level** (0–150%) and **Tune** (±12 semitones) per sound, remembered per kit. Tuning a synthesized sound scales every frequency in it (oscillators, sweeps and filters), so a cowbell goes up an octave at +12. **Reset mixer** puts the kit back to 100% and no tuning.
  - Storage: samples are kept in this browser only (IndexedDB) and never uploaded or committed.
  - Playback: the closed hat chokes the open hat, and accents play louder. The Linn-style patterns come mapped onto these rows.

**Variation** (all kits):
- **Trig conditions:** set **Tap sets** to *Conditions*, pick one, and tap hits to give them that condition (tap again to clear).
  - **A:B** plays on the A-th of every B loops of that row (1:2, 2:2 … 4:4, 1:8, 8:8).
  - **Fill / Not fill** follow the fill bars. **First / Not first** refer to the first loop after Play.
  - **25 / 50 / 75%** are chance.
- **Row length** (the menu at the end of each row, 1–16): a shorter row loops sooner and drifts against the others (polymeter). Its conditions count its own loops.
- **Evolve** (0–100%): each bar, a few ordinary hits are nudged a step, dropped, or joined by ghost notes on busy rows. Accents never change, and neither does your saved pattern; each Play starts a fresh variation.
- **Fill every 2 / 4 / 8 bars** turns on the last bar of each cycle (the light shows it). **Add fill** writes a snare/tom build on the last beat that plays only in fill bars.

The Latin kit:
- **Voices:** bass drum, conga low and high, bongo low and high, claves, cowbell, maracas and guiro
- **Rhythms:** Bossa Nova, Samba, Cha-Cha, Mambo, Rhumba, Beguine and Tango. Each is a one-bar, 16th-note pattern written from the standard groove of its style.
- **Editing:** tap a cell to cycle off → hit → accent, which makes the pattern "Custom". Tap a voice name to mute it.
- **Timing:** follows the clock (internal or MIDI clock) and the swing setting. Turning it on starts the transport, so it can play on its own.
- **Level**, plus optional **MIDI out** as General MIDI drum notes (usually channel 10)

### Sequencer
- **Length:** 2 to 6 steps
- **Each step:** a **pitch** offset of ±24 semitones (slider, ±1 / ±12 buttons, double-click resets to 0) and its own **volume** of 0–100%, which scales the note's velocity. 0% mutes the step, but the step still counts.
- **On/Off per step.** An Off step strikes nothing: it's a rest that still takes its turn, so the arp and sequencer move on. If the note before is still ringing, as Combo/VCA strikes can, the tail slides to the note the Off step would have played (the next arp note plus its offset) with a fast glide instead of a new strike. Oscillator 2 slides to its own version of that note, per its Same/Inverted setting. MIDI out gets only the rest.
- Each arpeggiated note moves the sequencer one step. A new chord resets both the arp and the sequencer to the start.

### Oscillator 2
- **Sound: West-coast / Upright bass.** **Upright bass** turns oscillator 2 into a plucked double bass. The note starts a touch sharp and settles (the pluck), there's a soft finger noise, and its brightness fades as it rings. Each note rings on until the next one, like a bassist's legato, or until it dies away (low notes ring longest). **Tone** goes from dark and thumpy to bright and twangy (aftertouch brightens it too), and **Decay** sets how long a low E rings. Choosing it drops oscillator 2 an octave if it's still up with the arp, and you can set Transpose further down. It keeps following the sequence (Same or Inverted), Level, mute-step slides and just intonation. The west-coast controls don't apply and are dimmed.
- A second voice plays on every arpeggiated note, using the same sequencer step as oscillator 1:
  - **Same:** plays the step's offset as it is (doubles the sequence)
  - **Inverted:** plays the offset flipped around the arp note, so +12 becomes −12 and +7 becomes −7
- Its own **wave**, **transpose** (±24 semitones), **detune** (±50 cents) and **level**. It also follows each step's volume.
- **MIDI Out Ch:** can send oscillator 2 to its own MIDI channel, so it can drive a second synth.
- Each step card shows the offset oscillator 2 will play for that step.

### Chord pads
- **Maj, min, min7, maj7** pads play a whole chord into the arpeggiator while you hold them (keys `1`–`4` work too). The chord is built on the **Root** you pick, in the keyboard's current octave.
- With **Latch** on, tap a pad and the chord keeps playing. Tapping another pad switches chords.
- Pads, keys and MIDI can all be used together. A note shared by two of them keeps sounding until both let go.

### Breakbeat slicer
- **Pads** (MPC style): **Pads** opens a pop-up of 16 pads, with pad 1 bottom-left as on an MPC. Each pad plays its slice the moment you touch it, and lights when the pattern plays it. **Bank** switches to slices 17–32 when there are more. On a computer, 1234 / QWER / ASDF / ZXCV play the pads, laid out like the grid.
  - **● Rec:** each hit is written into the tracker on the nearest step (a hit just before a step lands on that step), replacing what was there, at the hit's level. It starts the break and the transport if they aren't running. A hit you've just played isn't played again by its step a moment later. Notes on the Breakbeat's MIDI channel record too.
  - **Erase:** hold it (or hold Delete) and every row the playhead passes is cleared, MPC style.
  - **Undo:** takes back recorded hits and erased rows one at a time, up to 128.
  - Esc, **Close** or a tap outside closes the pads (and stops recording).
- **Switching slices off:** **Shift-click** (or **long-press**, on a touch screen) a slice in the waveform to switch it off, and again to switch it back on. It's hatched in the waveform and its rows in the tracker are struck through. While off it never plays: rows that use it act like empty rows, so the previous sound (or roll) carries on. **Evolve** and **Randomize** never pick it. Off slices are saved, but they reset if the slicing changes the number of slices.
- **Roll until next** (on by default): like the Polyend Tracker, a roll keeps retriggering through the empty rows after it until the next filled row (a slice or a Cut), wrapping round the pattern. **R** is the hits per 8th note: **1** = 8th notes, **2** = 16ths, **3** = 16th triplets, **4** = 32nds, **8** = 64ths (0 = no roll). Volume and note rolls ramp across the whole run, so Rv on a row followed by three empty ones fades out over all four. Note rolls stay within ±24 semitones. Chance is decided once, on the row itself. Rows the roll is carrying through are tagged **roll…** while they play. Turn it off for rolls that fill only their own step (then R1 and R2 are a single hit, since a step is a 16th: rolls start from R3).
- **What's playing:** with **Evolve** on, a row it changed shows a tag with what played instead: **→7** (slice 7), **R3** (a roll), **rev**. Each tag stays until that row comes round again, and Stop clears them.
- **Trim:** use only part of the loaded file. Drag on the overview strip to move the nearer edge, or use the **Start** and **End** sliders (a step per millisecond); the parts that are cut off are drawn faint. For finer work:
  - **Scrubbing:** a drag moves the edge by how far your finger travels, not to where it is, and the further you slide your finger up or down away from the strip while dragging, the slower it goes: full speed on the strip, half at about 50 px away, a quarter at 100, an eighth from 150 (as when scrubbing on an iPhone). Shift on a computer gives a tenth.
  - **Zoom:** a close-up of about 0.2 s either side of the **Start** or **End** edge (choose which), at full detail with ticks every 50 ms, so you can see the hit you're cutting at. Dragging in it moves that edge finely (its whole width is 0.4 s), with the same slide-away-to-slow-down; **‹ ›** nudge it 1 ms a tap, and keep going (faster after a moment) while held.

   **Trim (fold):** tap **Trim** to fold the trim controls away (it then shows the trim, e.g. "Trim: whole file" or "Trim: 0.120 to 2.800 s") and again to bring them back. The trim still applies while folded. It's remembered on this device and isn't part of scenes.

   The trimmed part is what gets sliced, played and fitted to the tempo, with tiny fades at the cuts so they don't click. **Whole file** undoes it. The trim is saved with your break and in scenes. Loading a new file starts untrimmed.
- **Evolve** (Breakbeat): each bar, swaps a few slices and adds the odd roll or reversed step; your pattern isn't changed, and the tracker tags each row Evolve changed. **Rolls** sets what its rolls are: **32nds** (fast retriggers inside the step, as before), **16ths** or **8ths** (a stutter: the slice again every 16th or 8th note, 2–4 hits, ringing on in between, while the pattern waits; tagged "16ths×3" or "8ths×3"). Untick **Reverse** and it never plays a step backwards.
- **Steps (fold):** the button above the tracker shows or hides its rows (the pattern keeps playing, and the waveform still shows the slice playing). It's remembered on this device and isn't part of scenes.
- **Break is:** how many bars the loaded (trimmed) break is, from **1 to 16**, so a 6-bar break is fitted to the tempo correctly. If the pattern is shorter than the break and still in order, it's lengthened to fit. **Steps** goes up to 128 (8 bars).
A drum break cut into slices and re-sequenced tracker style, for jungle, drum & bass and breakbeat.
- **Slicing:** **Equal** (8, 16 or 32 slices), or **On the hits**. Transient detection finds each kick, snare and hat (up to 32) and starts each slice just before the hit's attack. **Sensitivity** sets how many hits count: low gives only the big ones, high includes ghost notes.
- **Audio files:** every Load button (the break, Samples S1–S8, and the drum kit's Your samples) takes any kind of audio from your files: WAV (8/16/24/32-bit, float, µ-law, A-law, RF64, extensible), AIFF and AIFF-C, CAF, MP3, M4A/AAC, FLAC, Ogg Vorbis, Opus and WebM. Videos (MOV, MP4) work too: their sound is used. The browser decodes what it can, and the app reads WAV, AIFF and uncompressed CAF itself where the browser can't (Chrome has no AIFF; Safari refuses some WAVs). Formats no browser here decodes (WMA, AMR, ALAC in Chrome) say so when you load them.
- **Your breaks folder:** **Folder…** points the Breakbeat at the folder your breaks are in. Every audio file in it (and in its folders, hidden ones skipped) is listed in a menu next to it, in natural order (Break 2 before Break 10); pick one to load it, or step through them with **‹ ›**. **To S1** puts the one shown into the sample slot chosen in Samples (a second break for Tracks 2–4).
  - **Each break remembers its settings:** its trim, Break is (bars), Fit to tempo, Pitch, slicing (Equal/On the hits, Slices, Sensitivity) and moved slice points are kept by its path in the folder, so going back to a break brings them back. A break in a sample slot likewise keeps its trim, Fit, Loop is, Pitch and slicing.
  - **Reloading:** the break you were using, and the folder breaks in the sample slots, load from the folder again with their settings. In Chrome or Edge on a computer the folder itself is remembered (its name shows on the button); the browser may ask once a visit to let the page read it: tap the button. Until then, and in browsers that can't keep a folder (Safari, and on an iPhone or iPad, where you choose the folder, or the files, each visit), the copies kept in this browser play, so everything still sounds as you left it.
- **Break:** **Load break…** (or drop one on the waveform), or use the built-in synthesized **Demo break**. Your break is kept in this browser only.
- **Tempo:** **Break is** sets its length (1, 2 or 4 bars). **Fit to tempo** speeds it up or slows it down to the current BPM (and the pitch moves with it, the classic sound). **Pitch** adds an offset.
- **Pattern:** 16 or 32 steps of 16ths. Each row has:
  - **Slice:** an empty row lets the previous slice keep playing, as in a tracker.
  - **Vol**, and **Pitch** (±12).
  - **C (Chance):** 0–100% that the row plays. When it doesn't, the previous slice plays on.
  - **R (Roll):** hits per 8th note, 0–16: R1 = 8ths, R2 = 16ths, R4 = 32nds, R8 = 64ths, R16 = 128ths; odd numbers give triplet feels (R3 = 16th triplets). Patterns saved before R counted 8ths are converted (their R doubled), so they sound as they did. Types: **R** regular, **Rv**/**RV** volume down/up, **Rn**/**RN** a semitone down/up per hit, **RR** random notes.
  - **R % (roll chance):** 0–100% that the roll happens; when it doesn't, the step plays a single hit. Decided once on the row, so a roll held through the empty rows after it is all or nothing.
  - **FX:** a send into one of the Effects: **Dly** (Delay), **Rvb** (Reverb) or **Hrm** (Harmonizer). That step's hits (every hit of a roll) go into the effect at the row's **FX %**, on top of the section's own send, so you can throw a single snare into the delay, dub style. The sends follow the Breakbeat's mute and solo (and Break vol, on Track 1). **Rev** (Reverse) and **Cut** are still there too.
  - **FX %:** for **Dly / Rvb / Hrm**, how much of the step is sent to the effect, 0–100% (every time it plays). For **Rev** and **Cut**, the chance that they happen; when they don't, the step plays as normal (a Cut that doesn't happen lets the previous slice play on). C and R % are chances decided on their own, so a row can, say, always play, roll half the time and reverse a quarter of the time. Evolve's random reverses leave a step with a send alone.
  - On a phone the tracker scrolls sideways in its own box; on a narrower window a long pattern is listed in one column.
- **In order** places each slice on the 16th nearest where it falls in the break, so hit-sliced breaks still play back as recorded.
- **Hearing slices:** tap (or click) a slice in the waveform to hear it; the tracker isn't touched. Each row chooses its slice in its own Slice menu. **In order**, **Chop** (a jungle-style rearrangement), **Randomize** and **Clear** fill the pattern.
- **Moving slice points:** drag a slice line in the waveform (each has a small handle at its foot) to move where a slice starts: sideways moves it, up and down still scrolls the page. A line can't pass its neighbours (a slice stays at least 10 ms long). With the pattern in order, it follows the new points. The moved points are saved with the break and kept until you change the break, its trim or the slicing (Equal's count, or On the hits' sensitivity); **Reset slices** puts them back where the slicing had them.
- **Evolve** (0–100%): each loop, a few rows get a different slice, an extra roll or a reverse, and the odd slice lands on an empty row. Your pattern itself isn't changed.
- **Tracks 1–4:** **Track** above the tracker switches which track it shows (all four play at once; a track with something on it is underlined). **Track 1** is the break's slices. **Tracks 2, 3 and 4** play samples: each row's first column is a **Sample** (S1–S8) instead of a slice, with the same Vol, Pitch, C %, R, R %, Roll type, FX and FX % columns working the same way (Roll until next included). Each track is one channel: a new hit cuts off its last one, an empty row lets it ring on, and Cut stops it. **Clear** empties the track on show. **In order**, **Chop** and **Randomize** fill the track on show, leaving the others: on Track 1 from the break's slices, and on Tracks 2–4 from the sample chosen in **Samples**, using its slices (S1.1, S1.2…), or the whole sample if it isn't sliced. In order places each slice on the 16th nearest where it falls, over the sample's **Loop is** bars when it's fitted to the tempo (else over its own length at this tempo), repeating to fill the pattern. Evolve and the pads work on Track 1. The tracks are part of the pattern, so they're in the A–D patterns, sections and scenes. (An older pattern's Smp column moves to Track 2.)
- **Samples:** the eight slots, **S1–S8**, that Tracks 2–4 play (a vocal stab, a recorded hit, a riser). Choose a slot, then **Load…** a file, or record one in the Recorder and press **Put on track**. **▶** auditions it, **Level** sets its volume (per slot, saved) and **Clear** empties it. The sounds are kept in this browser and aren't part of scenes.
  - **A second break (or any loop) in a slot:** each slot can be trimmed and fitted to the tempo just like the break. **Trim…** opens the Breakbeat's trim controls on that sample (the **Break / Sample** switch at the top of Trim says which one they're editing; the overview, zoom, nudges and Start/End all work the same). **Fit to tempo** speeds it up or slows it down to the BPM (pitch moving with it), with **Loop is** saying how many bars the trimmed part is (the status shows its BPM as recorded); **Pitch** adds an offset. Then slice it (On the hits works well on a break) and play its slices on Tracks 2–4, alongside the main break. A new sound in a slot starts untrimmed; a bar-synced take put there from the Recorder is fitted to its bars. All of this is saved per slot on this device.
  - **Slicing a sample:** each slot can be cut into slices, like the break: **Slicing** **Whole** (the sample from its start), **Equal** (**Slices**: 8, 16 or 32) or **On the hits** (transient detection, with **Sensitivity**: low finds only the big hits, high ghost notes too). Its waveform shows the slices: tap one to hear it. On Tracks 2–4, the Sample menu then lists the slices under the sample: **S1.3** is slice 3 of S1, and **S1** is still the whole sample. A slice plays from its point and rings on until the track's next hit, as on Track 1; Reverse plays it backwards. Each slot keeps its own slicing (saved on this device, with the sample). If a reslice leaves fewer slices, a step on a slice that's gone plays the last one.
- **Break vol:** Track 1 (the break's slices) on its own, 0–150% (its effect throws follow it). Tracks 2–4 have their samples' Levels, and **Level** is the whole Breakbeat. Each pattern slot (A–D) keeps its own **Level** and **Break vol**, like its Evolve settings, so sections and songs change them with the pattern: switching slot (by hand, by a section change or in Song mode) brings that slot's volumes, and a song chain plays each slot at its own volumes from its first step.
- **Mixing:** it plays on the drum bus, so the drum delay/reverb sends apply. It has its own **Level** and follows the clock and swing.

### Effects
- **Limiter:** the main output ends in a limiter (a fast compressor holding peaks just under full scale, then a soft clipper that catches anything past it), so even Volume 100 with everything playing doesn't clip. Recordings of This app are taken after it. (Separate outputs skip it, as they skip the master compressor.)
- **Mute** (on each effect's row): silences that effect's output. Its sends keep feeding it, so unmuting brings its echoes, tail or harmonies straight back in mid-phrase, like muting an effect return on a mixer. The row dims while muted. Mutes are saved, and scenes include them.
- **Sends in every window:** the Arpeggiator, Chord rhythm, Mono synth, Percussion and Breakbeat windows each have a **Sends** row (Delay, Reverb, Harmonizer). They're the same sends as in this window: moving one moves the other.
- **Pre-fader sends:** every send (each section into Delay, Reverb and the Harmonizer) has its own **Pre** switch. Off (the default), the send follows the section's Level. On, it's taken before the Level, so turning the section down, even to 0, leaves that send untouched (for example drums heard only through the harmonizer, while their reverb still follows the fader). A section at Level 0 keeps playing while a pre-fader send needs it. Solo still applies to pre-fader sends.
A shared delay and reverb, with a **send** from each source into each one: **Arp**, **Chords**, **Perc** (the Percussion), **Break** (the Breakbeat) and **Bass** (the Mono Synth). The Percussion and the Breakbeat used to share one Drums send; a sound or scene saved before they were separate gives the Breakbeat the same sends (and Pre switches) as the Percussion, so it sounds as it did.
- **Delay:** **Time** synced to the tempo (1/16 – 1/2, including dotted and triplet values; it follows tempo changes and MIDI clock). **Feedback** sets each echo's level relative to the last. **Tone** darkens the repeats, and **Ping-pong** bounces echoes left/right.
- **Reverb:** convolution with a generated, decorrelated stereo tail that darkens as it decays. **Size** is the time to −60 dB (0.3–8 s), plus **Pre-delay** and **Tone**.

- **Harmonizer** (H949-style): a pitch shifter whose output feeds back through a short delay, so each repeat is shifted again. That gives spirals, rising and falling staircases, shimmer, and micro-pitch widening, great on percussion. Like the delay and reverb it's a send effect: **Perc send** and **Break send** start at 25%, the others at 0.
  - **Laid out like the H949's front panel.** The **Function** button picks one of two groups, each with four modes:
    - **Pitch change:** **Norm** shifts by splicing ~25 ms segments. **Extend** lets **Segment** set the splice length (longer is smoother on low sounds, very short is glitchy). **μPC** is micro pitch change, ±3% in 0.1% steps, for doubling. **Reverse** plays reversed segments (**Segment** = their length), pitch-shifted.
    - **Delay:** **Delay** is a plain delay. **Random** wanders the delay randomly over 0–25 ms (**Rate**) for double-tracking. **Flange** sweeps a delay against a fixed 12.5 ms copy (**Rate** = sweep time). **Reverse** plays reversed segments at the original pitch.
  - **Algorithm 1 / 2:** two ways of splicing. 2 is smoother but a little more chorused. Both are *deglitched*, as on the H949: each time a read head splices, it lands where its waveform lines up with the other head's, so the two crossfade in phase and the shifted note keeps its true pitch instead of thinning into beating sidebands. Reverse and Random keep their raw splices on purpose.
  - **Repeat:** captures the last Delay (or Segment) of sound and loops it, at the pitch ratio, and backwards in the Reverse modes. Press it again to let go.
  - **Main level** is the processed output. **Delay level** adds the plain delayed signal alongside it, for example a harmony plus a slapback. **Feedback** sends the output back in, so in the pitch modes each repeat is shifted again.
  - Knobs a mode doesn't use are dimmed, and the rest relabel themselves (Segment, Rate, L/R offset…).
  - **Pitch** is a continuous pitch **ratio**, as on the H949: ×0.250 (two octaves down) through ×0.500, ×1.000 and ×2.000 (an octave up) to ×2.500. ×1.498 is a fifth up. μPC shows its fine ratio the same way, from ×0.970 to ×1.030. **Spread** shifts left and right apart by up to 50 cents for the micro-pitch stereo spread. **Delay** (0–500 ms) is the time before the shifted sound and between repeats. **Feedback** sets how many times it goes round, shifted again each time. 
  - **Presets:** Micro-pitch double, Fifth up spiral, Octave up shimmer, Octave down thunder, Rising and Falling staircase, Glitch, Reverse shimmer, Backwards, Double-track, Slapback, Jet flange, and Harmony + slap.
  - It works like the original, by splicing a short delay line. Two read heads sweep through it and crossfade, which gives it a little of the hardware's grainy character.
### Input / output
- **MIDI Learn** (button at the top): every control you can assign gets a dashed outline. Click one (it blinks), then move a knob, fader or button on your controller, and that CC (on its channel) now drives the control. Click an armed control again to remove its mapping; **Clear all** removes every mapping; **Done** or Esc finishes. Mapped controls keep a ring around them, and their tooltip shows the CC.
  - **Sliders** follow the CC across their whole range, and **menus** step through their choices.
  - **Checkboxes** and **On/Off** switches turn on at 64 and up.
  - **Other buttons** (Latch, Tap, Rest, a sequencer length…) press when the CC goes past 64, so a controller button triggers them.
  - Mappings are saved on this device and don't change with scenes. A mapped CC is used only for its control.
- **MIDI In per module** (Sound & MIDI window): each module can be played live from its own MIDI channel. A channel set for a module goes only to that module. **Arp** (first in the row) plays the keyboard and arpeggiator: **Omni** gets every channel no other module has claimed, a channel number takes just that channel, and **Off** has the arp ignore MIDI.
  - **Chord synth:** plays live and polyphonically with the chord layer's analog or FM sound, holding until you let go. Just tuning sweetens what you hold.
  - **Mono synth:** last-note priority, like the real thing. Overlapping notes slide (legato), and letting go of the top note goes back to the one still held.
  - **Percussion:** General MIDI drum notes (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hat…) play the current kit's matching sounds, with its mixer levels and tuning.
  - **Breakbeat:** C1 (note 36) plays slice 1, C♯1 slice 2, and so on, for finger-drumming the break.
  - All notes off (CC 123/120) silences a module, and so does switching its channel off.
  - **Aftertouch** (on by default, with a **Depth**): polyphonic aftertouch shapes each note you press into. Keyboards that only send channel pressure work too, with the pressure applying to every held note.
    - **Chord synth:** each pressed note's own filter opens (up to two octaves at full depth) and gets a little louder. FM sounds get brighter and more bell-like instead.
    - **Mono synth:** the filter opens, following the note that's sounding.
    - **Arp:** pressing into a held key makes the arp notes that come from it louder and brighter.
    - **Chord layer from the keys:** keys held on the Arp channel also drive the chords, note by note. In **Pad** mode each held pad note's filter follows its own key's pressure. In **Rhythm** mode each hit of a pressed key's note is brighter and louder (FM sounds get deeper modulation).
- On-screen keyboard (click and drag), computer keys `A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L P ;`, `Z`/`X` to change octave, `Space` to play or stop
- MIDI in, with a channel filter or omni
- Built-in synth (wave, cutoff, volume) and/or MIDI out on any channel, timestamped for tight timing
- Cutoff sliders are logarithmic (60 Hz – 12 kHz), so each octave gets the same slider travel and the low end has more room
- **Looks:** **System 7** (the default), or the modular faceplate in **Light**, **Dark** or **Auto** (Auto follows your system setting). The theme is applied before the page draws, so there's no flash.
  - **System 7** is a black-and-white classic-Mac homage: a menu bar, pinstriped windows with close boxes, rounded buttons that invert, pop-up menus, X checkboxes, dithered scroll-bar sliders and dither patterns instead of color.
  - It uses a locally installed "Chicago" font if you have one (Chrome and Firefox; Safari only exposes system fonts). Otherwise titles fall back to Pixelify Sans. Small text uses the CC BY Geneva 9 recreation; see `fonts/README.md` for credits.
- **Sticky bar:** Play/Stop, Tempo and Tap stay at the top while you scroll, next to a jump chip for every window. A chip opens its window if folded and scrolls to it; a dot marks modules that are switched on.
- **Window order:** Clock and Keyboard first, then the arp (Arpeggiator, Sequencer, both voices), chords (Progression, Chord Rhythm), Mono Synth, drums (Percussion, Breakbeat, Song), and Effects and MIDI.
- **Touch:** double-tapping never zooms the page, so quick taps on buttons and cells register as taps. Pinch-zoom and scrolling still work.
- **Confirming:** buttons that erase something (mono **Clear**, **Clear samples**, a scene's **Update** and **Delete**) ask by turning into "Sure? …" for three seconds; click again to go ahead. Scene **Rename** edits the name in place (Enter saves, Esc cancels). There are no pop-up dialogs, because those freeze the page and would stop the music.
- **Help:** the explanations in each window are hidden to keep things compact. **Help** (top of the page) shows them all, and **Hide help** hides them again. The choice is remembered on this device. Hovering a control still shows its tooltip.
- **Collapsible windows:** click a window's title (or the triangle; in System 7, the close box) to fold it down to its header, which keeps its On button and main controls where it has them. **Collapse all / Expand all** are in the menu bar, and folded windows stay folded next visit.
- Settings are saved in `localStorage`

## Solo

The Arpeggiator (both voices), Chord Rhythm (with its pad and sub), Mono Synth, Percussion and Breakbeat windows each have an **S** button. In System 7 it sits at the right of the title bar. Solo one or more to hear only those. Their delay and reverb sends go quiet too, but echoes already ringing still finish. Alt-click solos a section on its own. The button still shows when a window is folded, and solo isn't saved: a fresh load starts with everything audible.

## Mixer

**Mixer** in the top bar opens a console over the page; press it again, **Close**, Esc, or click outside to shut it. Playback carries on while it's open.

- **Strips:** Arp, Chords, Mono, Drums and Break have a fader each, which is the same control as that window's Level slider. Moving one moves the other.
- **Returns:** Delay, Reverb and Harm have return faders. Master is the main Volume.
- **Meters:** each strip has a level meter with a peak marker, running from −60 to 0 dB. The readout above the fader shows the percentage and its dB value.
- **On/Off:** each track strip has an On button. It is the same switch as that window's On, so the two stay in step. A track that's off has its name dimmed.
- **M and S:** **M** mutes a strip. Mutes are saved with scenes. **S** is the same solo as the window's S button.
- **Reset:** double-click a fader to put it back to its default.
